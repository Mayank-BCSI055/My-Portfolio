## 1\. Introduction

This document defines the content requirements for the Personal Portfolio Website .

The purpose of this document is to define :

-   What content the website should contain
-   Where each piece of content should appear
-   How content should be structured
-   What information should be mandatory
-   What information should be optional
-   How project information should be presented
-   How professional information should be presented
-   How content should remain consistent with the personal brand
-   What content should be avoided
-   How content should be reviewed before publication

This document focuses on the information presented to visitors .

It does not define the technical implementation of storing or retrieving that content .

## 3\. Content Goals

The website content should help visitors quickly understand :

1.  Who I am
2.  What I do
3.  What technologies I work with
4.  What projects I have built
5.  What professional experience I have
6.  What I have learned
7.  How I approach development
8.  How visitors can contact me

The content should communicate professional credibility without becoming unnecessarily long .

## 4\. Content Principles

The following principles should guide all website content .

### 4.1 Principle 1 - Be Clear

Use simple language and avoid unnecessary complexity .

### 4.2 Principle 2 - Be Accurate

All professional , educational , technical , and project information must be truthful .

### 4.3 Principle 3 - Be Specific

Use concrete examples where possible .

Prefer :

Built a REST API using FastAPI and PostgreSQL .

over :

Worked on amazing backend technologies .

### 4.4 Principle 4 - Be Concise

Visitors should be able to scan the website quickly .

### 4.5 Principle 5 - Be Evidence - Based

Claims should be supported by actual projects , experience , results , or demonstrated work .

### 4.6 Principle 6 - Be Consistent

Terminology , Technology Names , Dates , Job Titles , and Descriptions should remain consistent .

### 4.7 Principle 7 - Be Human

The website should sound like a real developer rather than a corporate brochure assembled by seven committees .

## 7\. Content Ownership

The portfolio owner is responsible for ensuring that personal content is :

-   Accurate
-   Current
-   Professionally appropriate
-   Properly formatted
-   Updated when circumstances change

Content updates should be reviewed before being published .

## 8\. Personal Information Requirements

Only relevant professional information should be displayed publicly .

Required :

-   Full Name
-   Professional Title
-   Professional Contact Method
-   Professional Profiles

Optional :

-   Profile Photo
-   Location
-   Short Personal Detail

Sensitive Personal Information should not be displayed .

Examples of information to avoid :

-   Home Address
-   Personal Identification Numbers
-   Private Phone Numbers
-   Passwords
-   Private Credentials
-   Other Sensitive Data

## 9\. Name Requirements

The website should consistently use the professional name :

Mayank Baranwal

The spelling and capitalization should remain consistent across :

-   Header
-   Hero
-   About
-   Resume
-   Footer
-   Metadata where appropriate

**Priority :** P0

## 10\. Professional Title Requirements

The website should display a clear Professional Title .

Example :

Python Developer

The final title should accurately represent current skills and professional direction .

Avoid exaggerated titles that are not supported by actual experience .

## 11\. Hero Content Requirements

The Hero Section should provide the most important introduction .

Required Content :

-   Name
-   Professional Title
-   Short Introduction
-   Primary CTA
-   Secondary CTA

Example Structure :

Mayank Baranwal

Python Developer

I build practical software solutions with python ,

backend technologies , APIs , and modern web tools .

\[ View Projects \]

\[ Download Resume \]

The final wording should be refined during the content - writing stage .

## 12\. Hero Introduction Requirements

The Hero Introduction should answer :

-   Who are you ?
-   What do you do ?
-   What technologies or area do you focus on ?
-   What kind of work do you want to showcase ?

The introduction should ideally remain within :

-   1 - 3 short sentences

Avoid long paragraphs in the Hero Section .

## 13\. About Content Requirements

The About Section should provide more context than the Hero .

It should answer :

-   Who am I ?
-   What is my development background ?
-   What am I interested in ?
-   What have I been learning ?
-   What type of developer am I becoming ?

Recommended Structure :

-   Introduction
-   Background
-   Development Journey
-   Technical Interests
-   Career Direction

## 14\. About Content Length

The About Section should generally contain :

2 - 5 short paragraphs

or an equivalent structured format .

Avoid turning the About section into an autobiography .

Humanity has survived enough autobiographies already .

## 15\. About Content Guidelines

The About Section should :

-   Be written in first person where appropriate
-   Use natural language
-   Avoid excessive buzzwords
-   Mention relevant technical interests
-   Highlight genuine learning and development
-   Connect background to professional direction

Avoid statements such as :

I am a passionate ninja rockstar developer .

Prefer specific statements such as :

I enjoy building backend applications and learning how

APIs , databases , and software architecture work together .

The final statement must match actual experience .

## 16\. Skills Content Requirements

Skills should represent technologies that can be honestly discussed or demonstrated .

Possible Categories :

-   Programming Languages
-   Frontend
-   Backend
-   Databases
-   Tools
-   Version Control
-   Other Technologies

## 17\. Skill Naming Standards

Technology Names should use their official or commonly accepted names .

Prefer :

-   Python
-   JavaScript
-   FastAPI
-   PostgreSQL
-   Git
-   GitHub

Avoid inconsistent forms such as :

-   python
-   PYTHON
-   Fast Api
-   Postgres SQL
-   Github

unless the specific brand or technical convention requires it .

## 18\. Skill Proficiency Representation

The website should avoid misleading numerical skill ratings .

Avoid :

-   Python - 95 %
-   JavaScript - 87 %

unless there is a clearly defined and meaningful measurement system .

Prefer :

-   Primary
-   Working Knowledge
-   Familiar
-   Learning

or simply group technologies by relevance .

## 19\. Skill Evidence

Important skills should be supported by evidence where practical .

Example :

Python

│

├── Project A

├── Project B

└── Internship Experience

This allows visitors to see the difference between :

" I know this technology "

and :

" I have actually used this technology . "

## 20\. Project Content Requirements

Projects are one of the most important content categories .

Each selected project should communicate :

-   What was built ?
-   Why was it built ?
-   How was it built ?
-   What technologies were used ?
-   What problems were solved ?
-   What was learned ?
-   Where can it be viewed ?

## 21\. Project Summary Requirements

Each Project Card should contain :

-   Project Title
-   Short Description
-   Technology Tags
-   Project Status
-   GitHub Link
-   Live Demo Link where available

The Short Description should generally be :

1 - 3 sentences

## 22\. Project Detail Requirements

A detailed Project Page should contain :

-   Project Title
-   Overview
-   Problem Statement
-   Goals
-   Features
-   Technology Stack
-   Architecture
-   Implementation
-   Challenges
-   Solutions
-   Results
-   Lessons Learned
-   Screenshots
-   GitHub Link
-   Live Demo Link

Not every project needs every section .

The level of detail should depend on project complexity .

## 23\. Project Title Requirements

Project Titles should be :

-   Short
-   Memorable
-   Descriptive
-   Consistent

Example :

-   Task Management API
-   Personal Expense Tracker
-   Developer Portfolio

Avoid unnecessarily complicated names .

## 24\. Project Description Requirements

Project Descriptions should explain the project rather than simply list technologies .

Weak:

A Python Project using FastAPI and PostgreSQL .

Better :

A Backend API for managing tasks , users , and project workflows , built with FastAPI and PostgreSQL .

## 25\. Project Problem Statement

Where appropriate , Explain the problem the project addresses .

Example :

Problem : Managing tasks across multiple projects can become difficult

when information is scattered across different tools .

The problem should be realistic and connected to the project .

## 26\. Project Goals

Each detailed project may define 2 - 5 primary goals .

Example :

\- Provide a simple task management workflow

\- Build a reusable REST API

\- Practice database design

\- Implement authentication

\- Improve backend architecture skills

## 27\. Project Features

Important Project Features should be listed clearly .

Example :

\- User Authentication

\- Task Creation

\- Task Updates

\- Task Deletion

\- Task Filtering

\- REST API

\- Database Persistence

Features should describe actual implemented functionality .

## 28\. Technology Stack Content

Each project should list the technologies actually used .

Example :

-   FastAPI
-   Python

Database :

-   PostgreSQL

Tools :

-   Git
-   GitHub
-   Docker

Do not list technologies simply because they were considered or planned .

Example :

## 30\. Project Challenges

Important technical challenges may be documented .

Examples :

-   Database Design
-   API Validation
-   Authentication
-   Error Handling
-   Performance
-   Deployment
-   Responsive UI

## 31\. Project Solutions

For important challenges , Explain the approach taken .

Recommended Structure :

Challenge

     ↓

Approach

     ↓

Implementation

     ↓

Result

Example :

Challenge :

Implemented request validation and server - side validation

before processing the request .

## 32\. Project Results

Results should be factual .

Possible results include :

-   Completed Application
-   Working API
-   Successful Deployment
-   Reduced Manual Work
-   Improved Workflow

Avoid inventing numerical results .

## 33\. Lessons Learned

Examples :

\- Improved understanding of REST APIs

\- Improved error handling

\- Learned deployment basics

## 48\. Content Tone

The website content should have a tone that is :

-   Professional
-   Clear
-   Confident
-   Approachable
-   Technical
-   Honest
-   Human

It should avoid being :

-   Arrogant
-   Overly Corporate
-   Overly Casual
-   Exaggerated
-   Buzzword - heavy
-   Generic

## 49\. Writing Style

Content should generally use :

-   Short Sentences
-   Active Voice
-   Clear Technical Terms
-   Specific Examples
-   Consistent Terminology

Avoid unnecessarily complicated sentences .

## 50\. Technical Writing Guidelines

Technical Content should be understandable without removing important technical information .

Example :

Weak :

Implemented a highly scalable polymorphic distributed architecture .

Better :

Designed the backend as separate API and service layers

to keep business logic organized and easier to maintain .

Technical accuracy must remain the priority .

## 51\. Buzzword Restrictions

Avoid excessive use of terms such as :

-   Passionate
-   Innovative
-   Dynamic
-   Rockstar
-   Ninja
-   Guru
-   Cutting - Edge
-   Revolutionary
-   World - Class
-   Disruptive

These words are not forbidden , But they should only be used when they communicate something meaningful .

Specific evidence is stronger than adjectives .

## 75\. Content Error Handling

If content is unavailable :

Do not display :

-   undefined
-   null
-   N/A
-   Broken Image
-   Broken Link

Example :

-   Live Demo

should not be displayed if no live demo exists .

## 76\. Missing Content Strategy

If optional content is missing :

-   Hide Optional Field

rather than displaying :

-   Not Available
-   N / A
-   Coming Soon

unless that information is genuinely useful .

I am passionate about technology .

Innovative scalable cutting - edge next - generation solutions

Leaving old projects , roles , or resumes online without review .

## 94\. Final Content Principles

The following rules should remain true throughout the project :

### 94.1 Rule 1 - Tell the Truth

Never exaggerate skills , experience , or achievements .

### 94.2 Rule 2 - Show Evidence

Projects and Experience should support technical claims .

### 94.3 Rule 3 - Keep It Scannable

Visitors should understand the main points quickly .

### 94.4 Rule 4 - Write for Humans

Technical accuracy does not require unreadable writing .

### 94.5 Rule 5 - Keep It Current

Outdated content weakens an otherwise strong portfolio .

### 94.6 Rule 6 - Keep It Consistent

The portfolio , resume , and professional profiles should not contradict each other .

### 94.7 Rule 7 - Protect Confidential Information

Never publish information that should remain private .
